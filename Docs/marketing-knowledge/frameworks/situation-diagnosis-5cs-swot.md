## When to use

Use this framework before selecting an objective, channel, budget scenario,
or KPI. It is especially useful when the owner's stated goal is broad, such
as "increase sales" or "get more customers," because that request does not
identify where the customer journey is constrained.

The framework combines a short 5Cs interview with a compact SWOT summary.
The purpose is not to produce a comprehensive business analysis. It is to
identify the first evidence-supported bottleneck that a 12-week marketing
plan can responsibly address.

For an Egyptian SME case, use the owner's confirmed location, operating
language, business model, customer evidence, and constraints. The framework
itself is global guidance; do not turn it into an Egypt-specific market
claim without a separate Egypt or MENA source.

## Required inputs

Strategy should collect or explicitly mark as missing:

1. **Company and offer:** the product or service, current price or price
   range, reason to believe, capacity, stock or appointment constraints, and
   the action a customer takes to buy.
2. **Customer:** one priority customer group, the problem or need being
   addressed, location and language where relevant, and at least one
   owner-observed reason customers choose or reject the offer.
3. **Current journey evidence:** available counts or observations for
   discovery, enquiries or visits, completed purchases or bookings, and
   repeat behaviour. Do not invent missing baselines.
4. **Competition and alternatives:** the alternatives customers actually
   consider, including doing nothing, and an owner-confirmed difference that
   matters to the target customer.
5. **Collaborators and context:** dependencies such as suppliers, delivery
   platforms, partners, seasonality, or policy changes that could alter the
   plan.
6. **Execution readiness:** owner time, team skills, usable assets, available
   channels, budget mode, measurement access, and any fixed launch date.

The SBA market-research checklist supports checking demand, location,
saturation, alternatives, and pricing before assuming that promotion is the
answer. OpenStax places SWOT, objectives, target market, current situation,
strategy, budget, and controls in the marketing-planning sequence.

## Poor fit and stop conditions

Do not use a SWOT label as evidence by itself. "Strong brand," "high
quality," or "large demand" remains an assumption unless supported by
owner-confirmed records or eligible retrieved evidence.

Pause Strategy rather than selecting a marketing objective when:

- the offer, priority customer, or buying action is undefined;
- the business cannot fulfil more demand because of stock, staffing,
  response-time, licensing, safety, or service-quality constraints;
- the owner cannot distinguish attention, enquiries, purchases, and repeat
  behaviour and no baseline-establishment method is available;
- a launch has no confirmed offer, date, fulfilment capacity, or buying path;
- the proposed claim depends on missing local, legal, platform, or benchmark
  evidence.

The framework is not a substitute for financial, legal, clinical, or
operational diagnosis. Marketing should not be presented as the fix for a
non-marketing blocker.

## Diagnosis checklist

First label every input as a `confirmed_fact`, `retrieved_evidence`,
`assumption`, or `knowledge_gap`. Then run the 5Cs as a short decision
interview:

- **Company / الشركة:** What is sold, what can be delivered reliably, and
  what owner-confirmed advantage could affect customer choice?
- **Customers / العملاء:** Who currently buys, who should the next plan
  prioritize, what action shows intent, and what prevents purchase or repeat?
- **Competitors / المنافسون:** Which real alternatives compete for the same
  customer action, and what is known rather than guessed about them?
- **Collaborators / الشركاء:** Which supplier, platform, partner, or team
  dependency limits delivery or measurement?
- **Context / السياق:** Which current external condition could materially
  change demand, timing, trust, or the buying path?

Summarize only the strongest defensible items into SWOT: internal strengths
and weaknesses, then external opportunities and threats. Use that summary to
locate the first bottleneck:

| Bottleneck | Evidence pattern | Strategy response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness / الوعي | The priority audience cannot reliably discover or recognize the business, while the buying path is ready. | Consider an awareness objective and establish a relevant reach or discovery baseline. |
| Acquisition / اكتساب عملاء جدد | The business can fulfil demand and the buying path works, but too few new prospects or first-time customers enter it. | Consider acquisition and define a measurable first-customer action. |
| Conversion / التحويل | Qualified enquiries, visits, or started orders exist, but too few complete the paid action. | Consider conversion and diagnose the point of friction before adding traffic. |
| Retention / الاحتفاظ | Customers buy, but an owner-observable repeat or referral signal is weak. | Consider retention only when repeat customers can be recognized lawfully and measured. |
| Launch / الإطلاق | A genuinely new business, branch, product, or service has a confirmed date, offer, capacity, and buying path. | Consider launch with both discovery and first-customer checks. |
| Offer or pricing | Customers repeatedly reject the value, scope, or price, and promotion would amplify the same objection. | Record a blocker or owner decision; do not disguise it as a channel problem. |
| Trust | People discover the business but lack adequate proof, clarity, or reassurance to proceed. | Strengthen positioning and evidence before scaling promotion. |
| Operations | Demand exists but fulfilment, stock, staffing, response, or service quality cannot support it. | Create a blocking readiness gap outside the marketing plan. |
| Measurement | The relevant journey step has no observable baseline or tracking path. | Establish measurement first and avoid unsupported targets. |

When evidence could support more than one bottleneck, choose the earliest
unresolved constraint that would make a later-stage plan wasteful. Record
the alternative diagnosis as an assumption to test, not as a second primary
objective.

## How it feeds Strategy

The output is a compact diagnosis containing:

- the selected bottleneck and the evidence that supports it;
- the most plausible alternative diagnosis and what would disprove it;
- the owner-confirmed strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats that
  materially affect the next 12 weeks;
- the required owner decision, assumption, knowledge gap, or blocker;
- the candidate objective to test using
  `smart-objectives-funnel-mapping`.

Strategy should cite retrieved framework chunks for the method while citing
Business Profile facts separately as confirmed owner data. It must not cite
the framework as proof that a particular business has a specific
bottleneck.

## Assumptions, gaps, and blockers

- Use an **assumption** when a plausible interpretation can be tested safely
  during the plan, such as which of two customer objections is more common.
- Use a **knowledge gap** when external or baseline evidence is unavailable
  but the owner can gather it before approval.
- Use a **blocker** when proceeding would require inventing the offer,
  audience, buying action, readiness, legal permission, or a critical
  measurement path.
- If no eligible approved framework chunk is retrieved, emit
  `MISSING_FRAMEWORK_DATA`; do not replace the missing knowledge with model
  memory.
